## The default cost models per protocol version

The turbo README says to use "the correct semantics and default cost models depending on each epoch"; the
conformance corpus ships its models. What "default" means was settled against the reference reports: pragma's
build downloads every node's live report into `_site/reports/<scenario>/<node>.json`, and cardano-node's turbo
report gives the Haskell node's `budget_cpu` / `budget_mem` for all 3 997 scripts (Amaru's agrees with it on every
one). The models that reproduce those budgets bit-exactly are plutus' `defaultCostModelParamsForVariant` for the
semantics variant of each ledger language at each protocol version — the `builtinCostModel{A..E}.json` and
`cekMachineCosts{A..E}.json` of `plutus-core/cost-model/data/` — laid out in the ledger's `ParamName` order at
plutus 1.63's lengths (V1 and V2 332 parameters, V3 350):

| protocol version | V1 | V2 | V3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | A | A | — |
| 9 | B | B | C |
| 10 | B | B | C |
| 11 | D | D | E |

Fixture `src/consensus/__fixtures__/plutus-default-cost-models.json`, generated by
`scripts/ndc/plutus-default-cost-models.ts` from IntersectMBO/plutus `40b6705e34659f63ddeea460fecdb3fbf035b146`
(the checkout at `gerolamo-wt/ref-plutus`). Verification:

- the conformance corpus's `cost-models/protocol-version-10/v{1,2,3}.json` are byte-identical to the B / B / C
  derivations and `protocol-version-11/` to D / D / E (so the two scenarios use one notion of "default");
- every turbo budget: 3 997 / 3 997 equal to cardano-node's (`budget_agreement` in the manifest; pragma's build
  reports 0 budget disagreements for Gerolamo);
- against mainnet's on-chain parameters (Koios `epoch_params`): the protocol-9 lists equal epoch 520's V1 / V2 /
  V3 (166 / 175 / 251 parameters) exactly; the protocol-8 lists equal epoch 400's except the three secp256k1 V2
  parameters — `verifyEcdsaSecp256k1Signature-cpu-arguments` 35 190 005 (plutus) against 35 892 428 (mainnet),
  `verifySchnorrSecp256k1Signature-cpu-arguments-intercept` / `-slope` 39 121 781 / 32 260 against 38 887 044 /
  32 947. Evaluating with mainnet's parameters instead gave 3 990 / 3 997: the two V2 scripts calling
  `verifyEcdsaSecp256k1Signature` once differed by exactly 702 423 units (= 35 892 428 − 35 190 005), and five
  were "out of budget" at the README's 10 M memory limit (below).

The budget limit is mainnet's `maxTxExecutionUnits`, 10 billion CPU / 14 million memory, for both scenarios (the
conformance README's figures). The turbo README writes "10M mem", but cardano-node's and Amaru's reports carry
budgets above 10 million memory units for five scripts (10 369 230, 10 844 298, 11 057 305, 11 095 062,
11 296 656), so both references evaluated under at least mainnet's limit; with 10 M those five would be out of
budget here and could carry no budget to agree with.

Note, for the on-chain path: these are the scenarios' models, not the node's. Gerolamo evaluates with the cost
models of the ledger state; the plutus master values differ from 1.63's (what preview carries on chain) in the
Value builtins' parameters (insertCoin, unionValue, unValueData, scaleValue) and, at protocol 10, in
equalsByteString's and the integer division builtins' `c11`.

## uplc-conformance

How a vector runs (`src/conformance/ndc/uplcConformance.ts`):

- ids, groups, titles: the site's naming — the path under `v<lang>/` lower-cased with punctuation as `_`, plus
  `_v<lang>` (`builtin_parser_value_zero_asset_v3`), the group being the directories between the language and the
  case (`builtin/parser/value`), the title the case's name with punctuation as spaces; rows in id order, the same
  vector under two protocol versions by version;
- language and protocol version: the folders'; cost model: the corpus's for that pair (= plutus' default of the
  variant, above); budget limit 10 B / 14 M;
- evaluation: the result term printed as plutus prints it (`uplcPrint.ts`; the site compares up to whitespace), or
  "evaluation failure" / "out of budget" / "parse/decode error" (compared exactly); budget exact where a result is
  expected. Our own verdict uses the corpus's `.uplc.expected` and `.budget`: a `.budget` saturated at Int64 (the
  suite's counting evaluator has no limit) means "out of budget" under a real one, and so does a failing builtin
  whose cost alone exceeds the limit (the two `dropList … beyond_u64` vectors read "evaluation failure" in the
  corpus and "out of budget" in the site's expectations, which come from a run under the limit, as ours);
- the `.flat` form of a vector must also decode (as its language at its protocol version, the on-chain path) to
  the textual program, and `.flat.expected` to the expected output (empty files skipped: BLS12-381 points have no
  flat form).

### Bugs

**552 — a `value` literal was normalised instead of checked.** plutus master's text parser (`conValue`,
`Value.buildValueWith`) accepts a literal only in canonical form: keys of at most 32 bytes, quantities within the
signed 128-bit range, currencies and tokens strictly ascending, no empty inner list, no zero quantity; anything
else is a parse error. Ours built the value with `fromList` (duplicates summed, zeros and empty lists dropped,
keys sorted), so the 14 `builtin/parser/value` vectors expecting "parse/decode error" evaluated. Fix:
`uplcText.ts` reads a literal through `buildValueStrict` (the flat decoder's constructor; bounds and key lengths
checked as read). plutus 1.63's parser did normalise — its suite's `builtin/constant/value` vectors (`unordered`,
`duplicate-keys`, `zero-asset`, `empty-tokens`, `max-key-length-*`) expect the normalised value — so
`parseProgram(src, { valueLiterals: "normalise" })` keeps that behaviour and the 1.63 suite test uses it; the
master suite's six `builtin/parser/value` cases that were listed as text-parser gaps in `conformance.v4.test.ts`
now pass (1 003 of 1 011; the three remaining gaps are free-variable programs). Text only: on chain a Value
constant arrives flat and was already decoded strictly. Tests: `conformanceText.test.ts` "bug 552".

**553 — an unavailable Plutus Core version or builtin was an evaluation failure.** In plutus-ledger-api both are
decode errors: `mkTermToEvaluate` raises `PlutusCoreLanguageNotAvailableError`, a `ScriptDecodeError`, for a
version outside `plcVersionsAvailableIn`, and the flat decoder's `checkBuiltin` rejects a builtin outside the
language's set — which is how flat.ts has always reported them (`FlatError`), and how the Haskell runner behind
the site's expectations classifies them. `runTextVector` and `runConformanceCase` (conformance.ts) now return
"parse error" (printed "parse/decode error") for `programUnavailable`; bug 550's tests were updated to the
corrected classification. Tests: `conformanceText.test.ts` "bug 553". Nothing changes on the validation path:
phase 2 (`src/consensus/rules/phase2.ts`) decodes a script at the transaction's protocol version and an
unavailable version or builtin throws there as before. One difference with the Haskell node is left as it was
(outside this work's scope, flagged): phase2.ts answers a decode failure found at evaluation time with
`error` (no verdict), while the ledger treats `PlutusCoreLanguageNotAvailableError` from `mkTermToEvaluate` as
the script failing; no on-chain script has exercised it.

## uplc-turbo

How a script runs (`src/conformance/ndc/uplcTurbo.ts`): its protocol version from its epoch directory (≥ 644 →
11, ≥ 537 → 10, ≥ 507 → 9, else 8), its language's default cost model at that protocol version (above), the
CBOR unwrap and the flat decode (`src/consensus/plutus/flat.ts`, phase 2's path) once before anything is timed,
then the CEK machine within 10 B / 14 M. Timing: `Bun.nanoseconds()` around `evaluate` alone, 50 runs, `cpu_us`
= mean of the best 30; `mem_bytes` null (a garbage-collected runtime has no evaluation arena; the schema allows
null and the site then judges the CPU dimension only). The report carries `machine` and the 3 997 rows; the
decode time, min / median / max, the per-group summary, the worst CPU deviations, the budget agreement with
cardano-node's report and the setup text (with the process's CPU affinity) go to `manifest.json`.

### Warm-up, and what moves between runs

JavaScriptCore reaches its top tier for a script's paths after roughly a thousand evaluations of it and drops
back whenever a path it had not speculated on (a `case` term, a blake2b, a serialiseData …) turns hot, so a JIT
runtime's fifty runs of a script are measured in whatever state the machine is in. The warm-up is unmeasured and
its shape was settled by measurement, every variant kept as a flag of `uplc-turbo.ts`:

- a small set (the first 64 scripts plus the first 8 of each language, at least 3 passes and 3 s; the default,
  `--warmup-scope set`), then per script one unmeasured and 50 timed evaluations: 3 995 / 3 997, V3 10 / 10;
- every script once, then the small set: 3 994, V3 8 / 10;
- 10 passes over the whole corpus (`--warmup-scope corpus --warmup-passes 10`): 3 992 and 3 991 in two runs,
  V3 7 / 10 and 6 / 10 — the JIT specialises on the 3 987 V1 / V2 scripts and the ten V3 ones pay
  deoptimisation exits, so the more corpus-wide warm-up, the slower they got;
- one corpus pass, then 1 000 unmeasured evaluations per script (`--warmup-runs 1000`): 3 992, V3 9 / 10, twelve
  minutes — and the scripts it was meant to settle did not reach, inside the run, the state they show in
  isolation (`43711B35…-v3`: 693 µs after one warm-up run of its own, 1 007 after 50, 158 after 1 000, 857 in
  that run; budget 280 µs).

So the published protocol is the README's shape with the small warm-up, and the published run is the one made
after settling it (the sequence above is the record, not a selection). Across all of them the budgets never
moved; three to five scripts with ratios between 1.2 and 2.1 crossed the 2× line one way or the other — JIT
state, not evaluator speed. About forty seconds on two pinned cores.
